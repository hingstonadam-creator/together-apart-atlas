Project relevance: ARCH 6004 — Together, Apart.

A preliminary study of how a modular underground data centre could supply shared thermal infrastructure to the housing development and, in stages, the surrounding precinct.

Research question

How can an underground data centre provide shared thermal infrastructure for multigenerational housing, while allowing the energy network to expand with the surrounding precinct?

Building systems under consideration

Centralised hydronic heating and cooling with apartment-level fan-coil units. Mixed-use programmes include a bakery/deli, bars, communal laundry and studio. Thermal connections could be staged to future precinct buildings.

02 / Technical proposition

A shared thermal network

Liquid cooling is preferred for investigation because a water-based circuit can capture server heat at temperatures that may be more useful to building services than conventional air cooling. A heat exchanger transfers this heat to a central energy plant, where water-to-water heat pumps upgrade it before distribution. The heat still needs to be matched to demand.

  1. 01Data centre

    IT equipment produces heat during operation.

  2. 02Capture

    Liquid cooling carries heat to a heat exchanger.

  3. 03Upgrade and store

    Water-to-water heat pumps raise the temperature; a thermal buffer manages short-term demand changes.

  4. 04Distribute

    Hydronic circuits serve apartments and communal programmes, with staged branches for future buildings.

  5. 05Return or reject

    Closed circuits return water to the plant. Independent heat rejection remains necessary when heat demand is insufficient.

The system describes a potential infrastructure relationship, not an endlessly recycled energy loop. Its benefit depends on how much heat is used and the energy needed to recover and distribute it.

04 / Architectural implications

Infrastructure that links independent programmes

A shared section

A building section can trace supply and return from the basement plant to individual apartments, with distinct branches for public and communal facilities.

Room to expand

Staged connections could link the initial development to future buildings. The precinct network remains an open design opportunity until neighbouring loads are confirmed.

Different times and temperatures

Thermal storage and independent heat rejection address the mismatch between continuous IT heat generation and changing building demand.

Collective infrastructure, separate homes

The energy plant creates a shared technical relationship between otherwise independent domestic and communal programmes. It does not require residents to share a dwelling or daily routine.

05 / Limitations and open questions

What remains to be tested

  • Actual IT demand, rack density and server utilisation are unconfirmed.
  • Liquid-cooling equipment and recoverable temperatures require equipment selection.
  • Heat-pump COP and heat-capture efficiency are assumptions.
  • Annual building energy demands have not been verified through dynamic simulation.
  • The 190 MWh/year useful-heat figure is a design target, not measured performance.
  • Capital cost, maintenance, pumping energy and additional cooling requirements require assessment.
  • The future precinct network has not been sized against confirmed neighbouring building loads.
  • A complete water balance and whole-life carbon assessment remain outstanding.

Design conclusion

The preliminary model suggests that a modest data centre could produce substantially more recoverable heat than the initial development requires. The principal design challenge is managing thermal energy across different temperatures, times and users.

The proposal positions the data centre as a potential foundation for shared precinct infrastructure. Its environmental benefit depends on the heat actually used and the energy required to recover and distribute it.
