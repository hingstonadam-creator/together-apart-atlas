Data centre
heat recovery

Southwark Grounds   /   Adelaide, South Australia

The data centre extends the idea of shared resources within Together, Apart. Located beneath the development, it provides an opportunity to investigate how heat from computing equipment could support the homes and shared facilities above. This preliminary study considers how that connection could work and how it might extend to neighbouring buildings as Southwark Grounds develops.

Research question

How could a data centre contribute heat to the housing above while allowing the shared energy network to grow with the surrounding precinct?

Development and infrastructure

A shared building-services network

The proposal brings the data centre, housing and ground-floor facilities into a shared building-services network. Centralised hydronic heating and cooling, with apartment-level fan-coil units, would allow each household to control its own internal environment.

The bakery/deli, bars, communal laundry and studio provide further programmes to consider within this network. Their different patterns of use would need to inform how heat is distributed, with connections to future precinct buildings developed in stages.

A shared thermal network

From computing equipment to the homes above

I am investigating liquid cooling as a way to capture heat from the computing equipment and transfer it into a water-based system. A heat exchanger connects this circuit to the central energy plant, where water-to-water heat pumps raise the temperature for use in the building. A thermal buffer provides some flexibility between when heat is produced and when it is needed.

The network connects the basement infrastructure with the apartments and communal facilities above. Water returns through closed circuits, while independent heat rejection allows the data centre to operate when the building cannot use the available heat. The value of this arrangement depends on how much heat can be put to use and the energy required to recover and distribute it.

  1. 01Computing

    The data centre produces heat while operating. Capacity scenarios range from 75 kW initially to 150 kW, with potential expansion to 300 kW.

  2. 02Capture

    Liquid cooling carries heat through a water-based circuit to a heat exchanger.

  3. 03Upgrade and buffer

    The central plant uses water-to-water heat pumps; thermal storage helps manage short-term differences in demand.

  4. 04Distribute

    Hydronic circuits supply apartments and shared facilities, with staged connections for future buildings.

  5. 05Return or reject

    Water returns through closed circuits. Independent heat rejection remains available when demand is too low.

Infrastructure that links independent programmes

Shared services, independent households

Connecting through the building

The system introduces a relationship between the basement plant, individual homes and shared ground-floor facilities. Supply and return routes can be traced through the building section, with separate branches serving different programmes.

Allowing for future connections

The initial development could form the starting point for a wider precinct network. Providing for staged connections creates an opportunity to share heat with neighbouring buildings as they develop. The extent of that network would depend on their requirements and patterns of use.

Responding to changing demand

The data centre may produce heat continuously, while demand for space heating and hot water varies. Thermal storage can help manage short-term differences. Independent heat rejection remains part of the system for periods when the available heat exceeds what can be used.

Independent homes, shared resources

The energy plant gives the homes and communal facilities a shared source of infrastructure while allowing them to operate independently. This carries the project’s position beyond the dwelling arrangements. Residents can retain control over their own domestic environment while drawing on a resource shared across the development.

What remains to be tested

Further technical work

The study gives me a basis for considering how heat recovery could be incorporated into the proposal. Further work would need to establish the equipment, operating conditions, building demand and servicing requirements. These would determine how much of the available heat could be used and what would be required to deliver it.

Design conclusion

The preliminary study suggests that the proposed data centre could provide more recoverable heat over a year than the initial development requires. This shifts attention towards when the heat is needed, the temperatures required and the routes through which it could be distributed.

For Together, Apart, the data centre offers a way to connect independent homes and shared programmes through the infrastructure supporting them. Future precinct connections could extend that contribution beyond the site. The environmental value of the proposal would depend on the heat actually used, alongside the energy and equipment required to recover and distribute it.
